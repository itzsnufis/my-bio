about

Hardware first, then everything else.

I am itzsnufis — systems and hardware developer. I started out taking things apart to find out why they were slow, and never really stopped. These days that habit produces an operating system, a hand-built phone, and a workaround for every vendor tool that decided not to exist.

01

The hardware story

condensed, four steps

It began with a Raspberry Pi and a suspicious amount of free time. A board that boots Linux for forty euros is a standing invitation: sure, it runs an operating system — but how? That question is expensive to ask, because the honest answer is "write one and find out".

The phone came next, and it came from irritation rather than ambition. Pocket computers are the least repairable, least understandable machines most people own. So the plan was simple: take a Pi, add the parts a phone actually needs — radio, display, power path, battery — and build the logic around it. Nothing about that plan survived contact with reality intact, which is exactly what makes it worth doing.

Everything else on this site is the fallout: a kernel that keeps growing, a scheduler that keeps getting rewritten, and backend tooling written in the unglamorous style, because that is the kind of software I trust.

  1. step 01

    One single-board computer

    Bought to "play around with". Immediately compiled a kernel on it instead.

  2. step 02

    The phone idea

    If a phone is just a computer with opinions about antennas, build one that takes instructions.

  3. step 03

    Chip logic, by hand

    Level shifters, power rails, I2C and SPI buses, and a multimeter that has seen things.

  4. step 04

    Where it stands

    Boots, telephones, overheats politely. Antenna tuning still has its own agenda.

02

Off the clock

no screens involved
badminton

Twice a week, badly at first

Fastest way to find out which muscle groups a compiler does not train. Footwork is essentially cache locality applied to legs — get the position right and everything after it is cheap.

running

Podcast-length distances

Ten-ish kilometres, deliberately unhurried. Also the most reliable debugger I own: the answer to a stuck problem usually turns up around kilometre four, uninvited.

the bench

Screws and a drawer of maybe

Something gets opened every other week: keyboard switches, thermal paste, a fan that has learned to whine at exactly the wrong frequency. The parts drawer is labelled "will be useful", and it is — eventually.

working habits
  • Datasheet first, source code second. Always in that order.
  • Measure before optimising — guessing is just gambling with worse odds.
  • Dependencies are a subscription. Keep the list short and paid up.
  • Commit messages are documentation for someone with no memory: me, in six months.
  • Tests live where the code is supposed to be boring. That is the point of boring code.
  • Nothing is finished until it still works after a reboot and a power cut.
